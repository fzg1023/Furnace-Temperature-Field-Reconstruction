# Furnace Temperature Field Reconstruction

**Hybrid RBF-PR-TWSVD** for acoustic boiler thermometry — a dual-scale hybrid radial basis function
model with polynomial reproduction (Hybrid RBF-PR), fused with wall-temperature constraints
(TW, trust-weighted) and acoustic ray refraction correction, and solved by truncated singular
value decomposition (TSVD).

> Zhigang Feng, Qifei Li, *Meas. Sci. Technol.* **37** (2026) 255901,
> doi:[10.1088/1361-6501/ae7a9e](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6501/ae7a9e)

---

### File description

| File | Role |
| :-- | :-- |
| `pathT1.m` | **Fundamental data file.** Performs sensor placement, acoustic path creation, generation of ideal data and addition of errors. Its output feeds the subsequent reconstruction programs. |
| `TemperatureReconstructionData(.mat)` | Stores the data finally generated by `pathT1.m` (noise-free / noisy TOF vectors, geometry, etc.). |
| `Experiment_ShootRK4.m` | Main **Hybrid RBF-PR-TWSVD** reconstruction program (accounting for refraction); its original data come from `pathT1.m`. |
| `buildHybridRBFDesign.m` | Builds the hybrid RBF design matrix (fine-grid Gaussian + coarse-grid IMQ + first-order polynomial reproduction). |
| `buildRBFDesign.m` | Builds a single-scale RBF design matrix (used for shape-parameter search and baseline comparisons). |
| `solveSVD_RBF.m` | SVD / TSVD based solver for the RBF approach. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Every execution of `pathT1.m` **randomly regenerates** a completely different set of fundamental
> data (random noise and errors). To reproduce the reconstruction results presented in the paper,
> it is permissible to **skip this file** and directly run the subsequent reconstruction programs,
> which load the saved `.mat` data.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Acoustic thermometry fundamentals

The speed of sound in the medium is one-to-one related to the medium temperature:

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}} = Z\sqrt{T},
\qquad Z=\sqrt{\gamma R/M}
$$

The time of flight (TOF) along the $k$-th acoustic path is:

$$
t_k=\int_{l_k}\frac{1}{v(x,y)}\,\mathrm{d}l_k=\int_{l_k} f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}l_k,
\qquad f(x,y)=\frac{1}{Z\sqrt{T(x,y)}}
$$

### 2.2 Hybrid RBF-PR model

The measurement region is discretized on **two different scales**: a fine grid ($N$ nodes, Gaussian
kernel, capturing local detail) and a coarse grid ($M$ nodes, inverse multiquadric (IMQ) kernel,
providing global smoothness), plus a first-order polynomial reproduction term:

$$
f(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_n\varphi_n(x,y)+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\eta_m\delta_m(x,y)+P_1(x,y)
$$

$$
\varphi_n(x,y)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-x_n)^2+(y-y_n)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),
\qquad
\delta_m(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x-x_m)^2+(y-y_m)^2}+C},
\qquad
P_1(x,y)=a_x x+a_y y+a_0
$$

Substituting into the TOF integrals and discretizing yields the linear system $Ax=t$, with

$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}G & H & J\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
x=\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon^{T} & \delta^{T} & a^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
$$

The shape parameters ($\sigma$, $C$) are determined by a **fixed-step search**: 50 candidate values
are uniformly sampled in the interval from 0.5 to 2 times the average spacing of the corresponding
grid nodes, and the optimum is selected by minimizing the reconstruction RMSE.

### 2.3 Wall-temperature constraints (TW)

$D$ wall-temperature measurement points are deployed around the furnace perimeter. From the measured
wall temperature $T_{wall}$ the theoretical acoustic slowness is
$f_{wall}=\dfrac{1}{Z\sqrt{T_{wall}}}$, which is matched against the value computed by the model,
giving the constraint equation $E_{wall}x=f_{wall}$.

To satisfy the TOF and the wall-temperature constraints simultaneously, the following objective is
constructed:

$$
J(x)=\lVert Ax-t\rVert_2^2+\mu^2\lVert E_{wall}x-f_{wall}\rVert_2^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_{aug}=\begin{bmatrix}A\\ \sqrt{\mu}E_{wall}\end{bmatrix},\;
t_{aug}=\begin{bmatrix}t\\ \sqrt{\mu}f_{wall}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Here $\mu$ (the `alpha` variable in the code) is the trust weight of the wall-temperature observation
term. It is determined jointly by the **maximum-curvature criterion of the L-curve** and the
**minimum total residual** criterion:

| Boiler configuration | Max-curvature candidate | Selected value |
| :-- | :--: | :--: |
| 24 m × 24 m | $\alpha = 104.81$ | $\alpha = 138.95$ |
| 32 m × 24 m | $\alpha = 8.29$ | $\alpha = 19.31$ |

### 2.4 TSVD regularization and refraction correction

Singular value decomposition of the augmented matrix, $A_{aug}=U\Sigma V^{T}$, retaining only the
first $p$ dominant singular values (the truncation order is determined by cross-validation):

$$
x_{TSVD}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{u_i^{T}b_{aug}}{\sigma_i}v_i
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
T(x,y)=\left(\frac{1}{Z\cdot f(x,y)}\right)^{2}
$$

Because of the strong temperature gradient inside the furnace, sound rays bend according to
**Fermat's principle**; the curved paths are obtained iteratively from the Euler-Lagrange equation:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A([Start]) --> B["Acquire acoustic signals on 24 paths"]
    B --> C["Estimate TOF (time-delay estimation)"]
    C --> D["Reconstruct initial field T0 using straight paths L0"]
    D --> E["Build ray-bending model from T0, solve refracted paths L1"]
    E --> F["Reconstruct field T1 using curved paths L1"]
    F --> G["Solve refracted paths L2 from T1, reconstruct field T2"]
    G --> H{"abs(T2 - T1) &lt; theta ?"}
    H -- "No, set T1 = T2" --> G
    H -- Yes --> I["Output T2"]
    I --> J([End])
```

## 3. Simulation Setup

| Item | Setting |
| :-- | :-- |
| Boiler cross-section | 24 m × 24 m (8 transducers A-H); 32 m × 24 m (10 transducers A-J) |
| Effective acoustic paths | 24 |
| Wall temperature points | 8 (wall thermocouples, at 1/3 and 2/3 of each side) |
| Grid division | Fine grid 4 × 4 (Gaussian RBF centers), coarse grid 2 × 2 (IMQ RBF centers) |
| Sound source | White noise, 500 Hz - 2000 Hz, sampling rate 10 kHz, 10 000 samples |
| Delay / attenuation | 500-sample delay, attenuation coefficient 0.2; reference signal SNR 7 dB, delayed signal SNR -7 dB |
| TOF perturbation | $t = t\times(1+\beta N_{noise})$, $\beta\in[0,0.1]$; result tables are given at $\beta=3\%$ |
| Metrics | $E_{max}$ (K), $E_{mean}$ (%), $E_{rms}$ (%), central-zone $E_a$ (%), boundary-zone $E_b$ (%) |

**Theoretical temperature field models** (benchmarks used for the reconstruction comparison):

- 24 m × 24 m, unimodal skewed

$$
T(x,y)=800+\frac{600}{0.015\left[(x-15)^2+(y-15)^2\right]+1}
$$

- 24 m × 24 m, bimodal skewed

$$
T(x,y)=800+\frac{420}{0.018\left[(x-18)^2+(y-15)^2\right]+1}+\frac{360}{0.015\left[(x-6)^2+(y-9)^2\right]+1}

### 3.1 Cross-correlation time-delay estimation

At low SNR, spurious peaks appear in the cross-correlation function and obscure the primary peak,
which easily leads to time-delay misidentification. The paper introduces an attenuation factor to
suppress the noise interference of peak localization, making the primary peak sharper and its position
more accurate.

**Key findings**

- The conventional LSM-SVD and RBF-PR-SVD methods produce severely underestimated temperatures near
  the walls (reconstructed values as low as 500-600 K, with errors of 500-800 K).
- Introducing the hybrid RBF model reduces the maximum error from about 500 K to about 200 K;
  adding the wall-temperature constraint further reduces it to within 100 K.
- **With the refraction effect included**, the global error generally falls below 40-80 K and every
  metric becomes optimal, with the most pronounced improvement in the boundary zone $E_b$.
